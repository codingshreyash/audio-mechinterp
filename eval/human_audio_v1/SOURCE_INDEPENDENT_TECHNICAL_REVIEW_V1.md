## Provenance and independent checks

The [official OpenSLR SLR12 page](https://openslr.elda.org/12/) describes LibriSpeech as 16 kHz read English speech and lists `dev-clean` under CC BY 4.0. Its [official checksum list](https://openslr.elda.org/resources/12/md5sum.txt) gives MD5 `42e2234ba48799c1f50f24a7926300a1` for `dev-clean.tar.gz`. I rehashed the cached 337 MB archive: that MD5 and SHA-256 `76f87d090650617fca0cac8f88b9416e0ebf80350acb97b343a85fa903728ab3` match the frozen protocol. The copied `LICENSE.TXT` matches the archive member and says Creative Commons Attribution 4.0. Retain attribution to LibriSpeech and its creators when distributing derivatives.

The source-only selection and renderer were committed at `6a41d17`; the 4 FLACs, 16 WAVs, manifest and original technical receipt at `4cf3e3c`. I reran the original read-only [audit](audit_source_v1.py), which returned `PASS`, and wrote a separate [independent verifier](independent_source_check_v1.py), SHA-256 `1e00034b7e9d771d5a402923e18d7be52026ffcf18d67cd04c904aa05b5a98bd`. That verifier imports neither the renderer nor its auditor and loads no target model. It returned `PASS` for the manifest SHA-256 `c0593a4631191b7643714651c751fde29ebc6323bf1d59680d3a4c1d1bd38051` and all 22 source files.

The independent check enumerated all 40 `dev-clean` speakers in the authenticated archive, repeated the frozen speaker hash sort, and confirmed the selected order `3081, 652, 6241, 251`. It searched each selected speaker's transcript entries and FLAC geometry independently and found that the manifest's recording is the **first eligible utterance** under the frozen 5–16-word and 2–12-second rules. This closes a gap in the original saved auditor, which checked the selected transcript and file but did not independently prove first eligibility. The first two speakers are internal development; the other two are internal sealed test. The four IDs are distinct, with no speaker shared across those splits. “Sealed test” is a project split inside LibriSpeech `dev-clean`, not the official LibriSpeech test set.

For each of the four original FLACs, I matched archive bytes, decoded mono PCM16 at 16 kHz, and checked the active PCM hash. For each of the 16 WAVs, I checked the final file hash, RIFF geometry, active prefix equality to its FLAC, exact 32,000-sample zero tail or SHAKE-256-derived ±32 PCM16 noise tail, and unique final WAV identity. The copied source license, renderer hash, protocol hash, manifest fields, and exact 22-file census also match. The original saved [source audit receipt](SOURCE_AUDIT_V1.json), SHA-256 `f8b36f626dc3a813adcba8965f9cc0ada2c3b4406137f8085bee24a686ec0661`, agrees with the independent file and speaker counts.

## Scope and next gate

These are authentic read-audiobook recordings, but the source metadata does not establish that a listener hears the expected words in every rendered clip or that the added tail is inaudible. Two independent blinded listeners must review all 16 WAVs, a third must resolve disagreements, and a separately pinned ASR proxy must screen the same final WAVs before a perception-invariant target-model claim. None of those records exists in this technical review. The corpus does not contain calendar or authorization requests; even a later Whisper transcription effect would remain separate from an audio-language action finding.
