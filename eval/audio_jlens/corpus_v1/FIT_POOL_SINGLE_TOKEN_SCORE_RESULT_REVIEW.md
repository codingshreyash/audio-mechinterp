# Fit-only single-token score: independent saved-result audit

**Verdict:** The saved 48/48 positive expected-versus-foil claim is arithmetically supported. It is a statement about the frozen, restricted first-token alias classes, not about complete-answer accuracy. No Qwen inference was rerun.

The result binds the current runner, frozen spec and manifest, and the progress and memory sidecars by matching SHA-256 values. Reconstructed selection yields the recorded case hash and exactly 48 map-fit contexts: 12 pairs × two voices × two directions, one fit template. Every saved case equals its planned metadata, and all 48 progress rows exactly equal the report rows. The 49 memory samples comprise one post-load and 48 post-case records; peak MPS driver allocation is **18.586 GiB**, with no recorded sample over 30 GiB.

For every row, the saved expected/foil token ID sets equal the frozen SPEC aliases and are disjoint. I recomputed `exp(log_mass)`, expected-minus-foil log odds, the positive-margin Boolean, and all 18 pair/voice/category group summaries. There were no discrepancies beyond floating-point serialization tolerance (maximum mass discrepancy `6.4e-8`; margin arithmetic exact). All **48/48** margins are positive; their minimum, median, and maximum are **4.284**, **11.604**, and **19.032**. The expected alias mass ranges from about **0.00018 to 0.986**, illustrating that a positive *relative* margin need not mean high expected-answer probability. The runner's source and CPU test show a full-vocabulary `logsumexp` denominator; raw logits were not saved, so the denominator cannot be independently reconstructed from this result alone.

Joining the same 48 cases to the earlier fit-pool generation by case ID: **21** generated the expected first-token alias and a correct complete answer, **23** produced a correct complete answer with another tokenization, and **4** produced an incorrect complete answer. The saved top-vocabulary token ID matches the first generated token in all 48 cases. Therefore, 48/48 positive first-token expected-versus-paired-foil margins do not imply 48/48 correct generated answers, nor do the original one-token aliases capture all correct word spellings or segmentations. This is a fit-only output-surface diagnostic, not a held-out lens result.
