# Controlled audio-state patching: prior art and validation plan

Status: literature audit and proposed **future** protocol, 2026-11-18. This document does not modify the frozen v1 inputs, plan, code, or results. It uses no held-out observations.

## What v1 establishes

The [fit-only protocol](PROTOCOL.md) changes one spoken word, `red` versus `blue`, inside an otherwise byte-identical 16 kHz carrier, independently rendered with Piper and Kokoro. The Qwen2.5-Omni-3B Thinker, pinned at revision `f75b40e3da2003cdd6e1829b1f420ca70797c34e`, correctly answers both clean clips. In the [frozen results](patch_results_v1.json), swapping the corresponding **post-block audio-encoder rows** changes the complete generated answer to the donor color and reaches end of turn in 12/12 engine × layer × direction trials. The whole changed 2-second chunk also flips 12/12; a different chunk and a self-swap flip 0/12. These 12 trials reuse one lexical contrast, two generated voices, and three layers; they are not 12 independent word-pair replications.

This is a causal result for **that intervention in that model and task**, not yet a general lexical circuit, a speech-to-speech result, or a security finding. A post-attention row can contain information from elsewhere in its 2-second chunk, so a row index nominally aligned to the word does not prove that the underlying computation is local to those milliseconds. The wrong-chunk control is weak for temporal specificity: its median donor-minus-recipient Frobenius norm is about 3.1, versus 40.9 for the word-row swap (computed from v1 JSON). The original protocol also proposed wrong-layer and norm-matched random controls; they are absent from v1. No claim should silently count those controls as passed. The full answer is one color token plus end of turn, so v1 validates complete-answer change only for this deliberately simple task. Qwen2.5-Omni's Thinker generates text while a separate Talker can produce speech; this assay exercises the Thinker answer, not the Talker waveform. [Qwen2.5-Omni technical report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20215).

## Closest published work and novelty boundary

| Source | Causal intervention already shown | Relationship to v1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Beyond Transcription: Mechanistic Interpretability in ASR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15882) (2025) | Patches and ablates encoder/decoder components in Whisper and Qwen2-Audio, including a white-noise reference; studies acoustic/contextual transcription errors. | Establishes that audio-encoder patching itself is prior art. V1 differs in a matched one-word spoken contrast, row-window replacement, and an instruction-answer endpoint rather than ASR transcript restoration. |
| [Represented but Ignored: A Causal Account of Prosodic Underuse in Audio-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19211) (2026) | On four audio language models, including Qwen2.5-Omni, patches matched opposite-class **answer-position language-model states** and injects prosody directions. It distinguishes a moved answer-token score from an actual response change. | Very close methodological precedent for matched audio contrasts and causal readout. V1 changes audio-encoder temporal rows and tests lexical content plus complete answer, rather than prosody at the later answer position. |
| [Why Can't They Remember?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27039) (2026) | Uses donor hidden-state patching in Qwen2.5-Omni, Qwen2-Audio, and Kimi-Audio to rescue acoustic-memory errors; includes wrong-class and self-like donors. | Confirms that donor patching and Qwen2.5-Omni causal claims are established. Its target is a deep answer-position state in a multi-turn memory task, not a word-aligned encoder interval. |
| [Encoder-Side Neuron Identification and Amplification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11801) (2026) | Selects and amplifies audio-encoder neurons in three audio language models, including Qwen2.5-Omni, for non-semantic acoustic attributes. | Encoder-side causal editing is established; v1 is a matched lexical state swap, not neuron-level acoustic amplification. |
| [Uncovering Audio-Text Alignment through Cross-Modal Concept Neurons](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28189) (2026) | Steers and deactivates cross-modal neurons in Qwen2.5-Omni and other models, including audio-position-restricted controls. | Multimodal representation steering and audio-position controls are established. A lexical, time-indexed encoder swap remains a narrower possible extension. |

**Defensible present claim:** a fit-only proof of method for causally testing whether a controlled spoken-word change in Qwen2.5-Omni's audio encoder can change a whole Thinker answer. **Possible publishable contribution, contingent on validation:** a reproducible, temporally resolved intervention map of how a lexical contrast moves from waveform through encoder, connector, and language-model answer states, with transfer across unseen words, independently sourced voices, phrasings, and architectures, and with a security-relevant decision assay. The color flip alone is not novel enough to support that contribution. In particular, published prosody and memory work already caution against equating a decodable or score-shifting signal with robust behavior change. [Prosody study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19211); [acoustic-memory study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27039).

## Proposed locked replication, before any test run

First build a **dev set** from two new word pairs and two new voices. Use it only to debug waveform joins, time-to-row mapping, hooks, parsing, and compute. Then freeze code, model revisions, prompts, audio files and SHA-256s, patch sites, seeds, exclusions, metrics, and analysis before generating held-out patch outcomes. Record every generated clip, including failures; do not select only pairs that already flip.

The **held-out test matrix** has six word contrasts outside the fit and dev vocabulary × three held-out phrasings × four independent voice sources = **72 matched pairs / 144 clips**. Both patch directions give 144 directional cases per selected layer. A feasible split is two TTS engines/voice models absent from fit/dev and two human speakers absent from fit/dev. In the primary matched-waveform tier, record or synthesize the carrier prefix and suffix once per voice and splice only the target word, preserving identical PCM outside the declared interval. The human speakers also record separate natural full utterances for an external-validity tier; analyze those separately because they cannot satisfy byte-identical carriers. Choose word pairs from different semantic groups, matched where possible for syllable length, duration, amplitude, and answer-token complexity; publish the exact items and avoid `red/blue`. Three phrasings should ask for the same spoken-word recall with different surface wording and punctuation, with the answer format frozen. Varying a category or authorization rule is a **separate task**, not an unannounced phrasing variant of recall.

Primary hooks: predeclare encoder layers 0, 15, and 31 and their exact post-block tensor semantics, the word-row calculation, the connector output, and two fixed Thinker layers for a staged path analysis. Report the three encoder layers as an unswept family, not a selected best layer. If a second architecture is available, freeze its analogous sites using architecture rules before looking at outcomes; do not claim a shared layer number across models. The primary endpoint is the **entire decoded answer**, including end-of-turn, classified as donor, recipient, other, or malformed. The first-token donor-minus-recipient logit margin is secondary. Report clean audio and typed-answer accuracy, ASR transcript and human intelligibility checks, audio duration/energy/splice statistics, hook-call count, and every excluded or ineligible case. The primary denominator is all 144 planned directional cases per layer; also report a conditional rate among pairs where both unedited clips answer correctly. Failed clean pairs stay visible.

For each matched case and selected site, run these prespecified controls:

1. Clean recipient, donor, reciprocal patch, and self-patch; verify exact token IDs and end-of-turn.
2. Same-width wrong-time swap within the same chunk where possible, plus a separate non-word chunk. Report each edit's Frobenius norm. Scale a wrong-time delta to the main edit norm only as an additional diagnostic; flag out-of-distribution scaling rather than treating it as a perfect natural-state control.
3. Equal-norm random-direction edit at the **word rows** with multiple fixed seeds; a same-word/different-speaker donor; a different-word/different-speaker donor; and a word-window position shuffle. These distinguish generic perturbation, voice identity, and lexical content. Where time windows cannot have equal natural donor differences, match by sampled pairs and report residual imbalance.
4. Full changed-chunk swap and same-size mask/zero edit, to compare local sufficiency with broad damage; fixed wrong-layer interventions. Check unrelated response content and malformed-answer rate.
5. At least one boundary-offset sensitivity sweep on **dev only**; in test use frozen offsets, and report whether the result survives a ± one-row timing perturbation as a preregistered secondary analysis.

Success is not just a positive mean logit shift. The minimum evidence for a **general lexical-path claim** is: clean behavior adequate across every voice source; donor-class full-answer flips substantially exceed self, random, wrong-time, and same-word voice controls on held-out words and phrasings; both directions work; effects remain after grouping by pair and speaker; and an audio-encoder-to-connector-to-Thinker analysis identifies where that signal remains available versus where it actually affects the decision. Predeclare an effect threshold before test access, for example main flip rate ≥70% and each damaging-control flip rate ≤10%, with a cluster-bootstrap 95% interval for the paired main-minus-control difference excluding zero. Treat pair and voice as clustering units and show per-cell counts; 144 correlated directions are not 144 independent subjects. These thresholds are proposed design targets, not observed results or a guarantee of publication.

If this assay replicates, move to a separate, inert **decision** task that tests authorization or refusal, with no live tools. Its spoken contrast and correct decision must be defined before intervention. Track the complete decision and collateral failures, compare typed versus spoken paths, and only call it a security finding if the unedited system violates a stated security property or a realistic input can trigger a reproducible harmful decision under documented assumptions. A hidden-state swap requiring model access is a mechanism experiment, not by itself a deployable attack. The broader research question remains how audio representations produce security-relevant behavior across architectures; this simple lexical assay is the calibration step.
