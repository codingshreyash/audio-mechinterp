# Independent review: controlled red/blue audio patch

**Scope, 2026-11-18.** I audited the [fixed protocol](PROTOCOL.md), [v1 patch plan](patch_plan_v1.json), assembled [WAV manifest](../tts/outputs/controlled_pair_v1/controlled_pair_manifest.json), positive-control [answers](../tts/outputs/controlled_pair_v1/thinker_answers.json), [v1 patch code](patch_encoder_v1.py) and [results](patch_results_v1.json), and the later [v2 control plan](patch_plan_v2_controls.json), [code](patch_controls_v2.py), and [results](patch_controls_v2.json). I also independently reran the Piper layer-15 reciprocal word-local, self-patch, and wrong-chunk cases with a separate [replay script](independent_piper_replay.py) and [saved output](independent_piper_layer15.json). This is a **fit-only**, single-word, synthetic-speech mechanism pilot. It is not a held-out generalization or security finding.

## Input identity and acoustic control

I recomputed the SHA-256 of the fragment manifest, all four source fragments per engine, all four final WAVs, both patch plans, and the input manifest. The values agree with their manifests and result headers. In particular, the v1 result references plan SHA-256 `bb7bd6d02619e1a9a5ecac4c76ed496f3cb22e7d42a8d2d62447e6aa737e8293` and input-manifest SHA-256 `318807c92971babacad93b69b7d8478e29f397219da0cb9ed6e3134649aea98b`. The v2 plan pins the v1 result SHA-256 `6cfcd39fb57914559ee34fc9e21e45124d28692b9e49081ba9a51fea13e5368`; these hashes also match current files.

I independently decoded the PCM-16 WAVs. Red and blue have equal length within each engine, and **every PCM sample before and after the declared word slot is identical**. The Piper pair has 49,724 samples, slot `[18,082, 26,627)`, with 7,807 differing samples inside its 8,545-sample slot. The Kokoro pair has 101,840 samples, slot `[35,920, 61,120)`, with 17,282 differing samples inside its 25,200-sample slot. The remaining identical samples within a slot include padding/silence; the claim is exact byte control **outside** it.

This PCM control does not make the *model features* identical outside the slot. The pinned `WhisperFeatureExtractor` computes a log-mel floor using the clip-wide maximum. A CPU-only comparison of the processed valid feature tensors found differing mel frames far outside the word slot (excluding five mel frames on either side): 219 frames for Piper (max absolute feature difference 0.0673) and 348 for Kokoro (max 0.0378). The whole feature sequence can therefore carry small changes despite byte-identical outer PCM. The [ASR screen](../tts/outputs/controlled_pair_v1/asr_screen.json) recognized the two color words in all four clips, with Kokoro blue rendered as “Blues” by that ASR model; this is a quality screen, not a human-perception measurement.

## Row map and patch semantics

The pinned audio encoder uses 200-mel-frame self-attention chunks and two mel frames per encoder row; the feature hop is 160 samples at 16 kHz (10 ms). Recomputing the nominal indices gives:

| Engine | Valid mel frames | Encoder rows | Word-local rows | Changed chunk | Equal-count wrong-chunk rows |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| Piper | 311 | 156 | 57–83 (27) | 0–99 | 100–126 |
| Kokoro | 637 | 319 | 113–190 (78) | 100–199 | 0–77 |

These agree with all saved v1/v2 cases. The word window is selected from the known PCM slot and a **nominal** mel-to-row timing rule. It is not a forced alignment. The hook is on `thinker.model.audio_tower.layers[layer]` after each encoder block. The v1 code clones the recipient tensor, replaces only the selected rows with the saved donor rows, returns the modified tensor to the forward pass, and removes the hook afterward. It verifies donor/recipient tensor shapes and exactly one hook call per patched generation. Because these are **post-attention** states, even a nominal word-local row may contain information from elsewhere in its full attention chunk.

## Complete-answer outcomes

The clean answer gate passed **8/8** runs: four WAVs, each generated twice. Red produced token IDs `[6033, 151645]` and decoded “Red”; blue produced `[10331, 151645]` and decoded “Blue”. Every run reached normal EOS. In the v1 patch result, there are 12 cases (two engines × three layers × two reciprocal directions). All 48 patched generations had one hook call and normal EOS:

| V1 condition | Complete answers |
| --- | --- |
| Donor word-local rows | **12/12 changed to donor color** |
| Donor full changed chunk | **12/12 changed to donor color** |
| Donor equal-count wrong chunk | **12/12 retained recipient color** |
| Self-patch word rows | **12/12 retained recipient color** |

The wrong-chunk v1 control had much smaller donor-minus-recipient activation differences than the word rows, so its negative outcome alone would not separate **location** from **perturbation magnitude**. For example, Piper layer 15 had word-row norm 29.45 versus wrong-chunk norm 2.05. The v2 plan was fixed **after seeing v1 flips** and added equal-magnitude controls. I recomputed its counts from the saved complete answers: five norm-matched random matrices at word rows retained the recipient answer **60/60** times; adding the exact donor word-row delta at wrong-chunk rows changed to the donor **2/12** times; shuffling that delta across word rows changed to the donor **1/12** times. All 84 v2 outputs had one hook call and normal EOS. Effective BF16 delta norms differed from the donor word-row norm by at most 0.17% across these controls. These controls support signal and location/order dependence, while the three exceptions show that location/order effects are **not absolute**.

My independently written Piper layer-15 replay used the same pinned model and WAV hashes but did not import `patch_encoder_v1.py`. It independently derived rows 57–83 and 100–126, captured donor activations, and generated both reciprocal directions. It reproduced the v1 token IDs, EOS, answers, and Blue-minus-Red first-token margins exactly: word-local edits flipped both complete answers; self and wrong-chunk edits kept both recipient answers.

## Supported claim and next boundary

**Supported:** In this one fit-only red/blue question, swapping selected **post-block audio-encoder states** at rows nominally overlapping the changed word slot is sufficient to switch Qwen2.5-Omni-3B Thinker's complete text answer in both directions, across two TTS engines and three chosen layers. The independent Piper layer-15 replay confirms the saved outcome. Equal-magnitude random and displaced/permuted controls make a trivial perturbation-size explanation less plausible.

**Open:** The test does not identify a unique color direction, a pure word representation, an exact acoustic frame boundary, or a speech-to-speech mechanism. The source pair differs in one WAV slot, while preprocessing and encoder self-attention spread information beyond it. V2 controls were selected after seeing v1, and 2/12 wrong-location and 1/12 shuffled-delta cases also flipped. The pair uses one wording and two synthetic voices; no development/test pair or independent human-voice evaluation has been run. There is no confidentiality, integrity, or availability violation here. A stronger mechanism claim needs frozen new color/word pairs, new voices and phrasings, feature-path controls, and a direction-level intervention whose answer effect survives held-out tests.

## Addendum: input-feature splice control

The later [feature-splice plan](feature_splice_plan_v1.json) was fixed **after** the encoder patch and the discovery of mel-feature spillover, but before its [four saved results](feature_splice_results_v1.json). I checked the plan/result SHA-256 links and all four WAV hashes. Independently loading the pinned processor on CPU confirmed that each red/blue pair has exactly four batch fields: `input_ids`, `attention_mask`, `input_features`, and `feature_attention_mask`. The three non-feature fields are identical within an engine. The code clones the entire base batch and copies **only** the donor `input_features` slice for `word_only`; `outside_only` starts from the donor and restores the base slice. Thus tokens, masks, and every feature outside the assigned slice are preserved exactly in `word_only`.

The slice bounds recompute as Piper valid frames 311, `[108, 173)` and Kokoro valid frames 637, `[219, 388)`: `floor(slot_start_seconds × 100) − 5` through `ceil(slot_end_seconds × 100) + 5` inclusive, clipped to valid frames. These bounds and the recorded processed-audio hashes match all four cases. Even after BF16 conversion, 218 Piper and 348 Kokoro frames **outside** those slices differ between clean red and blue feature tensors; the splice separates their effect in this one question.

All four `word_only` hybrids answered with the **donor** color and normal EOS. All four `outside_only` hybrids answered with the **base** color and normal EOS. The clean base/donor answers in the same result retained their expected color and EOS. This strengthens the narrower claim that the selected word-adjacent **processed feature interval is sufficient** for the color decision here, while the clip-wide feature differences outside it are insufficient by themselves. The intervention is a hybrid log-mel tensor, not a physically replayable WAV or a guarantee that the interval contains only the spoken word. These controls still use the same fit-only pair, wording, voices, and model; they do not establish generality or a security violation.

## Addendum: audio-to-language connector patch

I audited the later [connector plan](connector_patch_plan_v1.json), [code](patch_connector_v1.py), and [four-case result](connector_patch_results_v1.json) without another MPS run. The plan and input-manifest SHA-256 values in the result match the current files. The code verifies each WAV hash, uses the pinned processor's audio-token placeholders, captures the output of `thinker.model.audio_tower.proj`, and applies the same single-call, clone-and-replace hook used for the encoder pilot. In the pinned model source, adjacent encoder rows are averaged before `proj`; the resulting rows are inserted in order into audio-placeholder token embeddings by `masked_scatter`.

An independent CPU processor check found 78 projected rows and 78 contiguous audio placeholders at token positions 18–95 for Piper, and 159 rows/placeholders at positions 18–176 for Kokoro. The projected-row windows recompute from the nominal centers as Piper **29–41** (13 rows, placeholder positions 47–59) and Kokoro **56–95** (40 rows, positions 74–113). The changed chunks are projected rows 0–49 and 50–99 respectively; equal-count wrong-chunk windows are 50–62 and 0–39. These match all four saved cases and the pinned stride-2 pooling rule.

All four reciprocal **word-local connector swaps** and all four changed-chunk swaps produced the donor color as a complete answer with normal EOS. The four original wrong-chunk swaps and four self-patches kept the recipient color. Twenty equal-norm random word-row edits (five seeds per case) also kept the recipient color. Every one of the 36 patched outcomes recorded exactly one hook call and normal EOS. The random edits' effective BF16 norms differed from their donor word-row target norm by at most **0.0034%**. The original wrong-chunk donor differences were much smaller than the word-row differences, so those four negatives alone are a weaker location control; the equal-norm random negatives address magnitude at the **same** rows, not displaced donor content.

**Claim boundary:** for this fit-only pair, the color-dependent signal is still sufficient at the audio-to-language connector: swapping a subset of projected audio embeddings changes the Thinker's complete answer in both directions. This traces a causal path through another model boundary. It does not identify a unique color feature or prove that those connector rows contain only the spoken word; each inherits full encoder-chunk context. New word pairs, speakers, prompts, and held-out tests are still needed before a general mechanism or security claim.

## Addendum: Thinker residual patches and 36-layer sweep

I independently checked the [three-layer plan](thinker_patch_plan_v1.json), [code](patch_thinker_residual_v1.py), and [results](thinker_patch_results_v1.json), plus the later [sweep plan](thinker_layer_sweep_plan_v1.json), [code](sweep_thinker_layers_v1.py), and [results](thinker_layer_sweep_results_v1.json). Both result files' plan and input-manifest hashes match the current files. The recipient and donor use the same token/placeholder layout. The word-local connector rows map to **Thinker audio-placeholder token positions 47–59** for Piper and **74–113** for Kokoro; all saved residual-patch and sweep cases use those positions.

The hook runs on a post-block `thinker.model.language_model.layers[layer]` tensor. During generation, it clones and replaces selected positions **only on the full-prompt prefill call**. When the decoder later receives one-token calls, the hook returns `None` and leaves them unchanged. Every saved patched run records exactly one modified prefill call and two unmodified decode calls, consistent with one answer token followed by EOS. The hook is removed after each run.

The three-layer pilot contains 12 cases and 120 patched generations. All 12 clean recipient answers were correct, and all 120 patched outputs reached normal EOS. At layers 0 and 17, word-local and all-audio-placeholder donor swaps each changed the complete answer in **8/8** reciprocal engine/direction cases across those two layers, while terminal-position donor swaps changed **0/8**. At layer 35, neither audio-position condition changed the answer (**0/4** each), while replacing the **terminal** position changed it in **4/4** cases. Across all three layers, self-patches, wrong-chunk audio-position swaps, and 60 equal-norm random word-position edits retained the recipient answer. The terminal-position swap is a broad positive control, not a word-local explanation.

The sweep result contains exactly the expected **144 unique cases**: 36 layers × two engines × two reciprocal directions. All 144 clean answers and all 144 patched outputs were complete and reached normal EOS; each patched output had one modified prefill call and two unmodified decode calls. Word-audio-position donor swaps changed the answer in **124/124 cases at layers 0–30** and **0/20 at layers 31–35**. This was a prespecified all-layer exploratory sweep after the three-layer pilot, not a held-out test. The change in outcome at 30→31 is sharp for this pair. It is not explained by vanishing donor differences: the mean donor-minus-recipient norm at the selected rows rises from about 436 at layer 30 to 538 at layer 31 and continues rising through layer 35.

**Layer-31 boundary:** The pinned Thinker config has 36 ordinary decoder blocks with `use_sliding_window=false` and `max_window_layers=70`; I found no configured attention-mode boundary at block 31. The observed threshold is a **causal sufficiency boundary for this intervention**, not evidence that a special layer-31 module exists or that the model first decides the color there. A causal decoder's terminal state has already read audio positions inside the current block; patching audio positions *after* that block can influence it only through later blocks. At layer 35 there are none, so the no-effect result for audio-position patches is structurally expected. At layer 31, four later blocks remain, and the saved result says they did not transfer this donor edit strongly enough to switch the answer. The increasingly large donor-state difference also means late swaps may be less compatible with the recipient's already-formed terminal context. A focused next check would patch the **terminal** residual at layers 30 and 31, alongside the audio-position patch, to test whether the recipient terminal state has become the decisive carrier. That would remain a broad intervention and would need new held-out pairs before a general claim.

## Addendum: posthoc audio-to-terminal handoff probe

That focused fit-only probe is now saved in the [handoff plan](thinker_handoff_plan_v1.json), [code](probe_thinker_handoff_v1.py), and [results](thinker_handoff_results_v1.json). I recomputed the plan, input-manifest, and prior word-sweep SHA-256 references; all match. The result contains exactly **144 unique engine × layer × reciprocal-direction cases**. All 144 clean answers are correct. Its 224 patched outputs all reached normal EOS, with exactly one modified full-prompt prefill hook call and two unmodified one-token decode calls.

Every layer has a terminal-position donor swap. At layers **0–30**, these changed the complete answer in **0/124** cases; at layers **31–35**, they changed it in **20/20**. An all-audio-placeholder donor swap was also tested at layers 28–35: **12/12** donor-color answers at 28–30, then **0/20** at 31–35. The layer-30 and layer-31 boundary controls retained the recipient answer under all **8/8 self terminal patches** and **40/40 equal-norm random terminal edits**; the random edits' effective BF16 norm was within **0.135%** of the donor terminal difference. The plan was fixed **after** observing the prior word-position sweep, so this is a posthoc diagnostic of one fit-only pair.

The first-token margins support the [boundary analysis](LAYER_BOUNDARY_ANALYSIS.md)'s **threshold-cliff** reading rather than a zero-to-nonzero influence claim. At layer 31, all-audio swaps still moved the Blue-minus-Red margin **toward the donor** by about 3.56–4.50 logits, but all four patched margins remained on the recipient side of zero. The earlier *word-only* layer-31 sweep likewise moved donor-oriented margins about 2.94–3.44 without changing answers, and its influence declined further at 32–33. By contrast, the layer-31 **terminal** donor swap crossed zero with modest donor-side margins: 0.25 (Piper red→blue), −0.75 (Piper blue→red), 1.125 (Kokoro red→blue), and −1.625 (Kokoro blue→red). At layers 32–35 the donor-side terminal margins strengthened. Thus the saved behavior is a switch in which **post-block patch site is sufficient to flip the answer**, with residual score sensitivity at late audio positions.

This is consistent with the terminal answer state becoming the effective carrier around this depth for this task. It does **not** show the exact natural computation that produced that state: a whole terminal-state swap is broad, earlier terminal swaps could be overwritten by recipient audio context in later blocks, and the donor states came from separate clean forwards. There is still no special configured layer-31 attention boundary, unique color direction, held-out replication, or security property violation.

## Addendum: block-31 attention/MLP mediation probe

I audited the later [fixed-before-this-run plan](block31_mediation_plan_v1.json), [hook code](block31_mediation_v1.py), and [four-case result](block31_mediation_results_v1.json) against the pinned decoder forward path. Its plan, input-manifest, prior handoff-result, and four case WAV SHA-256 values match current artifacts. The four cases cover Piper and Kokoro in both reciprocal color directions, with the same audio-placeholder word positions 47–59 and 74–113 and terminal positions 101 and 182. All **48 condition outputs** reached normal EOS; every registered hook ran once on full-prompt prefill and saw two later decode calls that it left unchanged. The clean answer and both self-patch controls reproduce the recipient's complete answer and first-token margin.

| Intervention | Donor-color complete answers |
| --- | ---: |
| Layer-30 word-position donor patch | 4/4 |
| Donor block-31 terminal attention output alone | 2/4 |
| Donor block-31 terminal MLP output alone | 0/4 |
| Layer-30 word patch **plus restore clean block-31 terminal attention output** | 0/4 |
| Layer-30 word patch **plus restore clean block-31 terminal MLP output** | 4/4 |
| Self attention/MLP terminal patches | 0/8 |
| Five equal-norm random terminal attention edits per case | 0/20 |

The random edits' effective BF16 norms differ from the donor attention-terminal difference by at most 0.0136%. The first-token margins agree with the answer counts: restoring clean terminal attention leaves all four margins on the recipient side; restoring clean terminal MLP leaves all four on the donor side. Donor attention output alone crosses the answer boundary in only two cases, so it is not generally sufficient by itself for this pair.

In the pinned `Qwen2_5OmniDecoderLayer.forward`, block 31 computes self-attention from the normalized input, **adds its output to the input residual**, then normalizes that sum, computes an MLP output, and adds that output. The attention hook edits the **first returned tensor** (the attention output), not an individual attention head or attention weight. Restoring the clean attention output while patching upstream audio rows also restores the terminal MLP's input in this block, because the upstream patch did not directly replace the terminal input row. The result supports a narrow causal statement: the **terminal attention-output route through block 31 is necessary for the layer-30 word-row patch to flip these four answers**. The MLP-output change is not necessary for those flips under this intervention. It does not prove that a unique head, a color-specific direction, or normal unpatched inference exclusively uses that route. The donor sublayer values were captured on clean `use_cache=False` forwards and injected into `generate(use_cache=True)` prefill; unchanged self-patch answers/margins support compatibility, but exact hidden-state equality across those modes was not independently measured.

The audit found one minor implementation issue that does not affect these fixed cases: the duplicate hook-name check could not detect duplicates. The check was changed to compare the number of dictionary entries with the number of requested edits after the saved run; no result or input artifact changed. A two-edit duplicate-name smoke call now raises the expected `ValueError` before model execution.

## Addendum: block-31 attention head-output scan

I independently audited the [head-scan plan](block31_head_scan_plan_v1.json), [hook code](block31_head_scan_v1.py), and [saved results](block31_head_scan_results_v1.json). The result's plan, input-manifest, and prior mediation-result hashes match the files; all four recorded WAV hashes match the manifest and current audio files. The cases cover Piper and Kokoro in both red/blue directions. Word-audio placeholder positions are 47–59 for Piper and 74–113 for Kokoro; terminal prompt positions are 101 and 182. Each case has 36 conditions, for **144 condition generations**, all with normal EOS. In the 140 conditions with a head prehook, it ran once on prefill and left two decode calls unchanged. The 68 combined upstream/head conditions also had one layer-30 prefill patch and two unchanged decode calls. The four layer-30-only conditions had the expected separate hook counts.

The pinned Qwen2.5-Omni attention code has 16 query heads of width 128 and two shared key/value heads. It concatenates query-head outputs immediately before a bias-free `o_proj`. The prehook replaces exactly the chosen 128-coordinate slice at the terminal prompt token; the optional upstream hook replaces word-audio rows in the output of block 30. Thus this tests a **query-head output contribution before projection**, not an attention pattern, key/value head, or isolated neuron.

The full-head controls reproduce the prior post-`o_proj` attention-output patch's complete token IDs **and first-token margins** in all four cases. The layer-30 word-row patch alone gives the donor answer in 4/4; donor all-head terminal patch alone gives it in 2/4; restoring all clean head slices after the upstream patch returns the recipient answer in 4/4; and the self all-head patch retains the recipient answer in 4/4.

Across the 16 individual heads and four cases, **0/64 donor-head-only patches** changed the complete answer to the donor, and **0/64 single-clean-head restorations** prevented the upstream donor answer. Head 3 produced the largest mean donor-oriented first-token margin shift (about +3.20 logits when donated and +2.75 logits toward the recipient when restored), but neither intervention crossed the complete-answer endpoint on its own. This is a posthoc scan of one word pair on two synthetic voices. It supports a distributed or thresholded contribution within this intervention, but no unique necessary or sufficient head, attention-pattern mechanism, held-out generalization, or security finding. A head selected from these results needs a frozen test on unseen words and voices.
