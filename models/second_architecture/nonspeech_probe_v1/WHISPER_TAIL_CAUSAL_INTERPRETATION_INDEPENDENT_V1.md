## What the intervention shows

For one previously selected engineered **non-speech** pair, adding a quiet 2-second tail changed multilingual Whisper Tiny's complete answer from `You` to `I` with the detected-language prefix unchanged. In three of twelve local donor-state transfers, swapping selected noise-recipient encoder rows for the zero-tail donor rows restored the complete `You` token path and EOS. These three are: boundary rows at the input to encoder block 0; tail-interior rows at the same site; and boundary rows after block 0. The corresponding identity, source-row donor, and norm-matched shifted-delta controls kept the noise-recipient answer, and all full-encoder donor controls reproduced donor complete paths in both directions. Thus the three **rescue** results are control-qualified for this pair.

The reciprocal zero-recipient to noise-donor local transfers induced `I` in **zero of six** tested site/region cells. This asymmetry matters: the intervention shows that replacing certain rows can restore the zero-tail answer in this one case. It does not show that those rows alone suffice to induce the noise-tail answer, or that a single localized feature determines the decision in general. At the final encoder norm, neither local region rescued the answer even though full-encoder donor swaps worked. That is consistent with dependence on other rows by that stage; it does not by itself locate a unique distributed circuit. After self-attention, information from the tail may already be present at many positions.

The complete token-path rescues are **not** full donor-state or score restoration. For example, the rescued post-conv boundary arm's first-step raw-logit vector differs from the zero-tail donor by a maximum absolute value of about `0.63`, while a full-donor swap matches that vector exactly. The earliest tested site is after the audio convolutions and positional embedding, so this experiment does not identify the preceding acoustic features or neurons that caused the effect.

Three late local target arms are not control-qualified. Their equal-norm shifted-delta controls changed the output: two reached the 128-token cap without EOS and one ended in a different answer. Those controls are artificial white-box state edits, and their degenerate generations are not attacker-reachable availability findings. The unchanged English-only checkpoint said `you` for both waveforms and had no changed outputs under any patch. Its `matches_donor` values are therefore tautological: they show no observed disruption, not answer transfer or cross-checkpoint replication. Language training/configuration differs between checkpoints, so this comparison does not isolate an architectural cause.

## Smallest useful next tests

1. **Freeze a seed-1 replication before opening its intervention output.** The V4 response to the second noise realization of the same `d08_beep_train_low` pair is already known (`You` versus `I`), so this is an out-of-sample *causal-patch* test, not a held-out discovery of the waveform effect. Keep the same 56-arm multilingual schedule, row regions, three sites, full-donor positives, controls, EOS requirement, and exact V4 baseline parity. Predefine success as rescue in the three seed-0 cells with controls qualified, and report all reverse and null cells. At the current run time this is cheap enough that reusing the full schedule is clearer than choosing cells after seeing seed 1.
2. **Then freeze one genuinely different sound family**, for example V4's `d08_band_noise_low` +32,000-sample seed-0 pair. Its complete multilingual answers are `you` for the zero tail and `Thank you.` for the noise tail; seed 1 did not show that contrast. Keep the 8-second source/tail row boundary and the same intervention logic and criteria. This tests whether the seed-0 patch pattern extends beyond beep-train sounds. Because this pair is already visible in V4, it is not a held-out behavioral discovery. A `d02_beep_train_low` pair would instead test a different duration/case within the beep-train family.
3. **Gate any human-speech/security claim on a new corpus** with independently reviewed intelligible recordings, invariant intended words across variants, native complete-answer differences, and fixed tool/action semantics where relevant. Repeat phase-specific causal controls there. The current non-speech hallucination case is a mechanistic pilot, not a security vulnerability.
