What these results establish

Scope: These are three small, revision-pinned ASR checkpoints on synthetic sounds. A transcript from non-speech is known prior art. This viewer shows observations, not a located circuit, a human-perception test, a speech-to-speech result, or a security vulnerability.

Silence itself produced you in both Whisper checkpoints. “Different from silence” is therefore more informative here than “nonempty.” The four multilingual Whisper differences and three Wav2Vec2 differences occur in the beep family; those are related cases, not independent replications.

How to read the internal fields

Language prefix ≠ transcript

Multilingual Whisper chose <|en|> in a separate language-detection forward. Its later generated lexical token is displayed separately. An English prefix alone is not evidence of speech.

Raw ≠ processed first token

“Raw top” is the highest saved logit before generation processing. “Processed top” is after generation processing and equals the first selected token in these records. Raw IDs 522 and 542 are on this checkpoint's suppression list. This does not isolate one processor as the cause of any full transcript.

Active geometry ≠ isolated sound

Whisper stored a 3,000-frame, 30-second mel input for each 2- or 8-second file. The measured processor mask has 200 or 800 active mel frames; the nominal encoder map has 100 or 400 active rows of 1,500. The pinned feature extractor's global max-relative mel floor and the model's ignored encoder attention mask mean nominal padding must not be assumed information-free. The descriptive records alone do not establish that padded rows caused a word.

CTC and evidence limits

Wav2Vec2 saves all CTC frame IDs and their collapsed text, but only a hash of the logits. The independent review verified the saved-ID collapse, not argmax against omitted logits. Whisper saves full first-step raw logits; processed-logit values and most encoder states are represented only by hashes or summaries.
