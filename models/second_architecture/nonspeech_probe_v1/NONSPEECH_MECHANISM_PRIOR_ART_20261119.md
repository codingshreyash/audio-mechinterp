# Prior art and claim boundary: non-speech audio mechanisms

19 November 2026. This is a source review, not a new model run. It covers the independently audited [multilingual Whisper Tiny result](INDEPENDENT_MULTILINGUAL_DESCRIPTIVE_V2_RESULT_REVIEW_20261119.md), [English Whisper Tiny and Wav2Vec2 controls](INDEPENDENT_COMPARATOR_V2_RESULT_REVIEW_20261119.md), and the phase-separated internal intervention whose independent result audit is pending. Only original papers and official implementation sources support the external claims below.

## What was known before this pilot

| Prior work | Established result and relevance |
| --- | --- |
| [Whisper paper](https://cdn.openai.com/papers/whisper.pdf) and [model card](https://github.com/openai/whisper/blob/main/model-card.md), 2022–23 | Whisper predicts task and language tokens as part of an encoder–decoder transcription format. Its model card explicitly warns that weak supervision can produce text absent from the audio. A language token is a task-prefix decision, not a spoken word. |
| [Whisper-AT](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.03183), July 2023 | Whisper's encoder representations retain information about non-speech background sounds; the authors trained an audio-event tagger on those representations. The broad idea that audio features can carry noise information is established. |
| [Barański et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.11378), January 2025 | Their Whisper large-v3 study tested 301,317 non-speech files including noise and silence and reported lexical hallucinations in 121,378 (40.3%). Thus “noise produces a transcript” is well established. Their prevalence is for their data, model, and decoding setup; it is not an expected rate for this pilot. |
| [Calm-Whisper](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/wang25b_interspeech.html), Interspeech 2025 | The authors implicated a small set of decoder self-attention heads in their non-speech hallucination setting and mitigated the behavior by modifying those heads. Internal components affecting hallucinations are prior art. |
| [Beyond Transcription](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.15882), August 2025 preprint | Applies probes, logit/encoder lenses, activation patching, and ablations to Whisper and Qwen2-Audio; includes non-speech detection and causal analysis of repetition hallucinations. **Audio mechanistic interpretability and ASR activation patching have already been done.** Its studied phenomena and interventions differ from this pilot's separate language-detection and transcription-pass donor matrix. |
| [AudioSAE](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05027), February 2026, and [Aparin et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07473), June 2026 | Sparse features in speech encoders and steering Whisper's hidden states have been used to detect or reduce hallucinations. The latter paper reports a non-speech hallucination reduction on its evaluation set. Therefore, changing a Whisper encoder state and changing hallucination behavior is not, by itself, novel. |
| [Hallucination Space Projection](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04561), September 2026 | Projects decoder hidden states away from a learned hallucination subspace and reports fewer non-speech hallucinations with accuracy tradeoffs. A decoder-side intervention also needs to be considered before assigning a sound-specific output to an encoder “circuit.” |

## Implementation details that change the interpretation

The official [Transformers Whisper generator](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/src/transformers/models/whisper/generation_whisper.py) and [generation code](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/src/transformers/generation/utils.py) apply token suppression during generation. The [configuration source](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/src/transformers/models/whisper/configuration_whisper.py) identifies `suppress_tokens` as a list of non-speech tokens. The audited multilingual run found that 16/20 raw first-step argmax tokens were on its pinned suppression list, while all 20 *selected* tokens were lexical. Raw logits are therefore not the emitted transcript or a direct measure of an unsuppressed acoustic preference. A controlled decoding-policy ablation is necessary to attribute a choice to that filter.

The [OpenAI transcription implementation](https://github.com/openai/whisper/blob/main/whisper/transcribe.py) combines a no-speech probability threshold with a low average log-probability condition to skip a segment. Transformers documents `no_speech_threshold` as a **long-form** transcription feature in its [Whisper generator](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/src/transformers/models/whisper/generation_whisper.py). Our short-form, pinned Transformers `generate` run is not evidence that OpenAI's `transcribe.py` silence filter was active or failed. Likewise, automatic selection of `<|en|>` for all 20 engineered sounds is not a multilingual transcript: all generated lexical text was Latin-script English.

## What this pilot contributes, and does not

The descriptive result is a tightly pinned **three-checkpoint, same-waveform comparison**: both Whisper Tiny checkpoints produced ` you` even for silence; multilingual Whisper differed from same-duration silence only on four beep inputs; Wav2Vec2 CTC produced nonempty strings on three beep inputs and empty output elsewhere. Silence's own word makes generic “hallucination from the beeps” an invalid explanation of the 16 non-beep Whisper outputs. The beep-family difference is a lead, not a population-level conclusion. The sources above establish non-speech hallucinations generally; this review did **not** verify a published result for this exact frozen 1-kHz beep train across these three pinned checkpoints, and that narrow absence is not a novelty claim.

The intervention design improves *local causal accounting*: it separately edits the automatic language-detection pass and transcription pass, uses full encoder donors, early nominal active/displaced rows, self/read-only/donor/random controls, and compares complete prefixes, text and EOS with a censor ledger. A positive transplant can show that a donor representation is sufficient **at the edited interface for a particular prompt**. It does not identify a neuron, show a uniquely acoustic mechanism, or prove that a real waveform can produce the same state. The 328-call result is still subject to its independent evidence audit; do not state its effect sizes as established here. The broader patching and steering concepts remain prior art.

## Route to a defensible new result

1. Finish the independent 328-call audit. Report the full denominator, failed and length-censored arms, exact native/donor/random outcomes, and whether a late-stage effect survives early-stage and phase-specific controls. Resolve the existing broad active-window patch with narrower, prospectively fixed temporal/component edits; bidirectional attention prevents treating one encoder row as one instant of sound.
2. Replicate on held-out sound families and independent recordings, with larger Whisper checkpoints and a second model family. Test decoder policy and no-speech handling separately from encoder edits. Evaluate speech quality cost so a trivial “suppress all output” intervention cannot count as a mechanism-specific remedy.
3. For a **security** claim, demonstrate an attacker-suppliable *waveform* causing a specified confidentiality, integrity, or availability violation under a concrete downstream system, with a baseline and safe controls. White-box transplantation alone is diagnostic. Targeted ASR waveform attacks already exist (for example, [Carlini and Wagner](https://nicholas.carlini.com/code/audio_adversarial_examples)); novelty would require a distinct mechanism or consequence beyond reproducing known hallucinations or adversarial transcription.
