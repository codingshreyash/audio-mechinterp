# Qwen audio front-end coupling: a no-weight finding

**Observation.** Two Piper recordings differ only in PCM samples `[18082,26626)` (1.1301–1.6641 seconds at 16 kHz). After both are padded with exact zero samples to 16 seconds, the pinned Qwen2.5-Omni-3B processor's `input_features` differ in 29,972 of 30,000 mel-frame columns. This includes the 28,400 columns after the 1,600 active frames. Their input IDs, attention masks, feature masks, and placeholder geometry match. The padded columns are a diagnostic of preprocessing; this check does not establish that the model attends to them.

**Cause in the pinned code.** Qwen's processor calls a Whisper feature extractor. At the [pinned Transformers revision](../qwen_omni/pins.json), `src/transformers/models/whisper/feature_extraction_whisper.py` lines 159–165 clamp every bin to `max(log_mel) - 8` separately for each recording, then map `(x + 4) / 4`. The original blue word supplies the larger maximum (raw log-mel 1.6545179 at frame 128); the red recording's maximum is in a shared suffix (1.3852977 at frame 216). This changes the clamp floor from −0.65367556 (red) to −0.58637047 (blue) in the final features. Their exact floor difference, 0.06730509, explains the uniform offset across all padded columns and much of the identical waveform outside the word. The raw log-mel differences before this clamp are confined to frames `[112,168)`.

**Prospective control, checked without model weights.** Apply the same gain `0.73` to samples `[18082,26626)` in **both** PCM streams, round to signed 16-bit samples, and leave all other samples unchanged. The common suffix then supplies the maximum for both processed recordings. Independent processor checks found the two feature arrays bitwise equal outside mel frames `[118,159)`; exactly 41 columns differ, and all token and mask geometry still matches. This calibrated pair is a new stimulus selected from preprocessing measurements before any model answer. The original pair remains the observation of global front-end coupling.

The pinned Qwen processor (`processing_qwen2_5_omni.py`, lines 195–203) uses the Whisper extractor; its audio encoder (`modeling_qwen2_5_omni.py`, lines 717–749 and 867–904) splits 200 mel frames per attention chunk, so the calibrated difference lies inside the first chunk. Its convolutions can enlarge the support slightly before attention, and attention can spread it across that chunk. Thus 41 differing mel columns do not prove a localized final representation.

This is a **preprocessing mechanism and experimental confound**, not a model-answer result or a security vulnerability. Attenuating the two words could affect intelligibility; the next pilot must require complete EOS-terminated red and blue baseline answers before interpreting any activation swap. Human intelligibility would need separate listening evidence.
