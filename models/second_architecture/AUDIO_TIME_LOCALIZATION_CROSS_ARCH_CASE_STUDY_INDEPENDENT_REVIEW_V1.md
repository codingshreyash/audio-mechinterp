# Independent review: two-ASR audio time-localization case study

19 November 2026. **Decision: GO for the corrected, FIT-only synthesis in commit `c5639c2`; HOLD for held-out generalization, speech-assistant mechanisms and security claims.** I reviewed `AUDIO_TIME_LOCALIZATION_CROSS_ARCH_CASE_STUDY_V1.md` against the independent Whisper first-pair, object-pair and spread-versus-use result reviews, the independent Wav2Vec2 baseline, GroupNorm reconstruction and causal result reviews, and the saved Wav2Vec2 causal JSONL. I ran no model inference and opened no locked-test material. An initial draft omitted the first Whisper random-control deviation, omitted the unmatched Wav2Vec2 P/Q magnitudes and linked a not-yet-built Wav2Vec2 explorer; the author corrected all three before this GO. The original draft remains in commit `ebb518f`.

The Whisper reviews support 12/12 donor word-window target-only complete-transcript changes across three sites for `silver`/`teal`, 12/12 across three sites for `plate`/`cup`, and 16/16 across two later sites in the follow-up. At those later sites, the active-audio complement, equal-width prefix and equal-width postword patches each produced 0/16 target-only changes. The first assay's eight random arms used only four distinct tensors due to reverse-direction seed reuse, correctly preserved in the corrected synthesis. The first outside-word difference is described as **observed after encoder block 0**, with no attribution to attention alone or a unique subcomponent; this matches the saved-state reviews.

The Wav2Vec2 baseline review establishes that the stock processor makes off-word input samples differ before the model. Under shared outside-word preprocessing, first raw-convolution off-word differences are exactly zero and first GroupNorm off-word differences are 2.15–4.80. The separate formula review independently rehashed all relevant captures and exactly matched the saved normalized tensors with PyTorch GroupNorm. The synthesis correctly calls the shared-stat calculation algebraic and stops short of claiming an effect on decoding.

I independently counted the completed Wav2Vec2 causal JSONL's 224 patch rows: word-window W gave target-only transfer in **4/4 eligible directions per site**, with only Piper `plate`↔`cup` in S/C eligible; W also matched the model's complete donor transcript in **16/16 directions per site**. The latter includes donor recognition errors and is not intended-word accuracy. Prefix/postword controls preserved the full recipient transcript in **64/64** total site-direction arms. Random arms gave **0/64** target-only donor transfers but **11/64** complete-transcript changes to other spellings. Exact self and full-donor logit controls passed in all 32 arms each. These counts agree with the independent result review; Wav2Vec2's full active-audio complement has not yet been tested. The corrected case study also notes that P/Q donor edit norms were not matched to W.

The document's inference boundary is appropriate: both studies use the same two already-inspected FIT word pairs and synthetic voices; Wav2Vec2 primary comparisons are only one lexical pair in one voice repeated under correlated normalization modes. The cross-architecture observation is an internal ASR state-sufficiency result plus differing earliest observed spread stages. It does not establish a unique circuit, an attacker-reachable waveform effect, a general audio-language model rule, or a security vulnerability. The prospective explorer is now described as under development rather than represented as an existing Wav2Vec2 viewer.
