# Next experiment: where acoustic variation changes an audio model's decision

18 November 2026. **Design only.** No candidate model was run for this memo. The current [Voxtral fresh-word Track B assay](VOXTRAL_FP32_TRACK_B_ANSWER_LINK_PROSPECTIVE_V3.md) asks whether a recipient-specific derivative and a projector-state edit predict a **complete generated word answer** for four new word pairs in Piper and Samantha speech. That is a useful answer-link check. It does **not** vary noise, speaking rate or recording channel, and it does not compare those effects across model families. Its two voices test a small voice contrast, but the target edit remains an internal state edit, not a waveform a user could supply. Therefore a positive Track B result would not, by itself, test the broader hypothesis: **the same intelligible spoken request can be transformed by different audio pathways into different internal states and consequential decisions.**

## Why this follows the completed evidence

The [Whisper/Wav2Vec2 cross-architecture case study](AUDIO_TIME_LOCALIZATION_CROSS_ARCH_CASE_STUDY_V1.md) already shows why the question is mechanistic: with a one-word PCM swap, the first *observed* spread outside the word was in Whisper encoder block 0, but in Wav2Vec2 it was before the transformer, at stock utterance normalization or time-wide GroupNorm under a shared-statistics counterfactual. Word-window donor patches controlled complete transcripts in those FIT cases; outside-window patches did not. That does not prove the outside state is irrelevant. Wav2Vec2 matched the **model's donor transcript** in 16/16 per site, yet only 4/16 planned directions met the intended-word rule, because clean ASR itself erred. The [Voxtral development audit](VOXTRAL_DEV_TRANSFER_INDEPENDENT_RESULT_REVIEW_V1.md) supplies a second warning: a random edit at the projector caused lexical loss in 56/96 directions against an 18/96 ceiling. A changed output alone is not a specific mechanism. The [Qwen synthesis](AUDIO_LANGUAGE_MECHANISM_SYNTHESIS_V1.md) likewise records a strong input-specific local Jacobian but a failed corpus-fitted reusable map. These are bounded FIT/development observations, not attack rates.

## Prospective assay, separate from frozen Track B

**Question.** Hold the words and task fixed while changing one acoustic factor at a time. Does the complete behavior change, where does the earliest measured difference appear, and can a targeted state replacement restore the clean behavior more often than magnitude-matched controls? Run the same *question* in three different paths: `openai/whisper-tiny.en` (encoder-decoder ASR), `facebook/wav2vec2-base-960h` (CTC ASR), and pinned Voxtral Mini 3B (audio-language answer). Compare boundaries within each model; hidden coordinates and generation steps are not interchangeable across models. A later speech-to-speech/action model is a separate extension, not a result of this assay.

**Inputs and split.** Reuse the local Piper, Samantha and Kokoro renderers and the [word-disjoint V2 fixture](word_disjoint_v2/README.md) for engineering and acoustic-level calibration only, subject to its still-required 32/32 audited listening gate before candidate inference. Those WAVs, including the 16 unused by Voxtral V3, are **not** a fresh sealed cross-model test: their words and render recipe are exposed, and Wav2Vec2/Whisper V2 protocols plan to use all eight pairs. For the claim-bearing test, freeze new word-disjoint utterances *and source speakers* in a separate manifest before any target-model output is inspected. Keep every derivative of a source clip in the same split. Use at least two independently produced voices in development and a distinct held-out speaker/engine, with a genuine human speaker as a separate external-validity stratum if recorded; never describe Samantha or Kokoro as human. The exact pair count, voice IDs, transforms, seeds, model revisions, stopping rules and decision thresholds belong in a separately reviewed prospective protocol. No clip replacement based on clean model accuracy.

**One-factor contrasts.** For each clean utterance, make prespecified (1) additive background noise at fixed signal-to-noise levels and seed, (2) pitch-preserving time stretch at fixed rates, and (3) a fixed microphone/channel filter or impulse response. Keep the text and sentence context identical. A naïve resample also changes pitch, so it cannot stand in for an isolated rate factor. Preserve the clean version, waveform hash, sample rate, duration, measured level/SNR and transform parameters. Include a transform-only control on nonword audio or silence where meaningful. Human blinded listeners record the complete words and intelligibility *before* model results are opened; independent ASR may assist quality triage but is not a proxy for human equivalence. Report unintelligible and excluded clips in the planned denominator.

**Observational map.** Save raw PCM, processor output, aligned frame/time masks, early and late encoder states, the connector/projector state when present, and complete model outputs. Measure where a clean-versus-transformed difference first appears and its size in and outside the speech window. This is descriptive only. Rate changes alter time geometry: align by known transform mapping or validated phonetic landmarks, never by assuming frame `i` still denotes the same sound. A global noise/channel edit has no literally unchanged outside-word PCM, so do not apply the old word-swap locality inference to it. Keep Wav2Vec2 stock preprocessing as the primary path; its shared-statistics mode is a diagnostic counterfactual.

**Causal check.** At one prespecified site per model, patch the transformed run with the matched clean state in (a) the aligned speech window, (b) its active complement, and (c) the full state; include self/zero, reverse direction and independently sampled equal-norm random edits. Compare the realized edit norm, exact source geometry, and complete output. For Voxtral, require clean no-hook/zero token parity and donor full-state parity before interpreting a switch; reuse Track B's strict full-answer/EOS and alias-coverage discipline rather than treating a score crossing as an answer. For Whisper and Wav2Vec2, score the **whole transcript**, with intended words and unchanged context separately from the weaker model-donor-transcript match. A targeted rescue that is comparable to random edits is a failed specificity test. Patching demonstrates a white-box causal boundary; it does not show that a transformed waveform can induce the patched state.

## Decision and security relevance

The primary result is a table by **source utterance**, speaker, transform and model: human wording, clean and transformed full outputs, first measured divergence, targeted/random/full patch outcomes, and failures. Correlated variants and reverse directions are not independent samples. A stronger mechanism claim requires (i) preserved human wording, (ii) a reproducible complete-output difference or stability under the acoustic change, and (iii) a targeted, parity-valid rescue or transfer that beats its matched controls on held-out source utterances and speakers. A null result still narrows the boundary: it says which perturbations and models did not exhibit a decision change under the frozen conditions.

The **security step** is subsequent and narrower: in an unmodified speech assistant, show an attacker-suppliable, human-intelligible waveform changing a consequential authorization, disclosure or tool proposal under a specified inert harness. Log the raw model text/function/audio branches and any dispatcher decision. A transcription error or internal patch alone is a robustness/mechanism observation. The [VoiceChat source map](SPEECH_BRANCH_MODEL_MAP_AND_ASSAY.md) identifies a later native action path, but its live parity and human-QA gates remain separate; do not infer an executed action from a proposed tool string or mix pass-1 tool generation with pass-2 spoken output.

**Novelty boundary.** This is a prospective cross-architecture, controlled-acoustics extension of the local FIT studies. It is not a first-ever audio interpretability method, a general audio-to-language lens, or a discovered vulnerability. The decisive contribution would be a reproducible link from an ordinary acoustic change, through a measured and causally tested internal boundary, to a complete and security-relevant model behavior.
