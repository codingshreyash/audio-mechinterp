## Memory decision

The failure shows that the frozen 24-GiB MPS ceiling blocks **loading**, before any forward pass or VJP. It does not establish the peak memory needed to finish loading, generate, or retain three FP32 teacher-forced gradients. Raising the ceiling to 28 GiB offers at most 4 GiB additional allowance; 30 GiB offers at most 6 GiB. Those numbers alone do not show that either setting will finish the remaining checkpoint or the more demanding VJP. On a 48-GiB unified-memory host, a 30-GiB MPS allowance also leaves only 18 GiB nominally for the OS, CPU-side staging, and other processes; the 7.158-GiB process RSS peak and the error's 7.74-GiB “other allocations” are different observations and cannot simply be added or treated as independent headroom. The saved evidence has no system memory-pressure trace. **Neither 28 nor 30 GiB is justified as a safe, sufficient executable amendment from this attempt alone.** Do not disable the MPS high-watermark guard.

For a new, separately reviewed protocol, prefer a higher-memory GPU or an explicit CPU/offload load-and-VJP feasibility design that preserves FP32 target, hook, and saved-parity semantics. CPU/offload may reduce MPS peak but can raise RSS and exceed the 300-second whole-attempt gate; those limits need prospective measurement and a new fixed attempt. A narrower alternative is a separately gated **load-only** resource characterization on this 48-GiB host, with a bounded MPS ceiling, live RSS/system-memory-pressure/swap sampling, and a stop before forward or VJP. Such a diagnostic would estimate loading headroom only and would not establish scientific feasibility. Any new cap, placement, time limit, or attempt changes committed source identity and requires a fresh independent review and GO. This memo authorizes none of them.
