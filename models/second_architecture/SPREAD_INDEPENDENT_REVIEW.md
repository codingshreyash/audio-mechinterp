# Independent review: Voxtral fit-pair representation spread

I audited [voxtral_representation_spread.py](voxtral_representation_spread.py) and its [saved fit-only result](runs/voxtral_representation_spread_fit_v1/piper_red_blue_20261118T063119808668Z.json) (SHA-256 `6f1f80115ccc62fc6fbd1716c5c1c064b8eedcbb82cef942554330b1e03856a1`). The result's script, smoke-script, dependency, manifest, model-snapshot, and WAV hashes match current pinned files. I ran the three CPU shape/partition tests and independently recomputed front-end mel features from the saved WAVs; I did not run MPS or open development/test model results.

The original Piper Red/Blue WAVs each contain 49,724 mono 16 kHz PCM frames. Their int16 samples are **exactly identical outside** the saved word slot `[18082, 26627)` and differ inside. The two processor records have identical 390 input-token IDs, identical user instruction text, 375 audio placeholders, and `[1,128,3000]` feature shape. Thus neither prompt wording nor differing PCM outside the slot explains the feature difference. The script's partitions recompute: 56 nominal word mel frames, 255 other real-audio frames, 2,689 nominal-padding frames; and 9 nominal word connector rows (13–21), 30 other real-audio rows, 336 nominal-padding rows. These connector boundaries use only the direct convolution/projector receptive field, before bidirectional encoder attention.

The installed Whisper feature extractor pads each clip to 30 seconds, computes log-mel values, then applies `max(log_mel, clip_max − 8)` and divides the result by 4 after an offset. I independently reproduced that CPU calculation. Before the **clip-wide clipping step**, the Red/Blue log-mel arrays are bitwise identical at all **2,944/2,944 frames** whose centered STFT windows avoid the word slot. Red's log-mel maximum is `1.385298` at frame 216; Blue's is `1.654518` at frame 128, which lies in the word slot. Their maximum difference is `0.269220`. In the silent padded region, each of 128 mel bins therefore shifts by about `0.269220/4`; the predicted frame L2 difference is `sqrt(128)×0.269220/4 = 0.761470`, matching the saved padded-frame value. My independently computed post-clipping per-frame L2 vector matches all 3,000 saved values exactly.

After preprocessing, **227/255** outside-word real-audio mel frames and **2,689/2,689** nominal-padding frames differ by more than `1e−6`. The saved squared-L2 shares are 38.8% in the nominal word window, 1.9% in other real audio, and 59.3% in nominal padding. That large padding share reflects thousands of identically shifted frames, not evidence that padding is semantically more important. The 30-second feature difference is explained by a **global front-end normalization/clipping floor**, despite local PCM substitution. It must not be attributed to an internal model circuit.

The passive `multi_modal_projector` hook captures one `[375,3072]` output per clip and returns no replacement. Hooked and unhooked greedy answers have identical token IDs: Red `[12846,2]` and Blue `[24851,2]`, both with EOS. The projector-output difference also spans nominal padding (336/336 rows nonzero; 39.6% of saved squared L2), but those rows already received different mel values before the encoder, and the pinned audio encoder uses bidirectional attention without a padding mask. This result does **not** isolate how much spread is due to normalization, convolution, attention, or projection. All reported partition counts and squared-L2 shares recompute from the saved vectors. Peak observed MPS driver allocation is about **9.93 GiB**, below the 30 GiB cap.

This is one fit-only synthetic word pair and a passive representation observation. A later causal claim about word localization would need controls that account for clip-wide feature normalization and the padded region, plus an intervention with complete-answer outcomes. No such claim follows from this spread artifact.
