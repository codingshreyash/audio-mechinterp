## Blocking review-order failure

The handoff and two-phase builder require **both** initial 48-clip exports to be complete before any reference script is revealed. `evaluate()` validates each comparison only against that reviewer's own first-export time. I constructed complete 48-record fake exports with primary first export at 07:00, its reference comparison at 07:01, secondary first export at 08:00 and secondary comparison at 08:01 UTC. `evaluate()` returned `human_reviews_complete_source_eligibility_recorded`, 48 reviewed arms and 12 eligible sources. The primary reviewer could therefore see references while the secondary reviewer was still listening, contrary to the frozen blind procedure. The test uses consistent per-record timestamps and passes all other validations.

The same missing chronology affects sparse third review. With a real disagreement flag in fake records, I set both initial reviewers' comparison timestamps to 08:00 but the third first export and comparison to 06:00 and 06:01. `evaluate()` accepted `third_reviewed=true` and one disputed arm. A third review must occur only after the initial comparisons establish its exact flagged population. Require every primary/secondary comparison record to be at or after **both** initial export timestamps, and every third-review start, submission and comparison to be at or after both completed initial comparison exports or an explicit authenticated flag-freeze timestamp. The comparison export schema currently has no completion timestamp, so the later production entry point should bind when both comparison exports were locked. The pure auditor can at least reject the chronological counterexamples above.

## Comparison form replay and production binding

`human_qa48_compare.js` stores progress under a key made from only `first_export_sha256` and reviewer ID. Its resumed-row check compares the opaque ID and four choice fields, but not the immutable row content or a digest of the rendered comparison template. A changed template for the same first export can resume old marks on a new page. The final Python `validate_comparison()` rejects changed reference fields if the exported rows came from a prior template, so this is **not** a demonstrated accepted forged report. It is still a form-provenance and reviewer-experience gap: bind storage to an exact comparison-template digest and reject stale immutable row fields. The source handoff already correctly says production remains HOLD until a builder reconstructs and authenticates every rendered page, queue and WAV. That production binding must cover comparison pages as well as first-pass pages.
