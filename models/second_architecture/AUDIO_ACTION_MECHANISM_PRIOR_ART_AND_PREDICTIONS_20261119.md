# Audio evidence, latent representation, and action selection

19 November 2026. Source-grounded research memo, not a model run or security finding. The links below are the authors' papers; this memo distinguishes their readouts from complete generated behavior and from our unrun VoiceChat assay.

## What the nearby literature has already established

| Primary result | Intervention and endpoint | Boundary for this project |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Causal Localization of the Refusal Direction in Audio Language Models](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22260) | Across five audio-language models, the authors fit refusal-linked directions and ablate them at the audio interface and selected LM layers. On Qwen2.5-Omni, LM layer 16 changes their first-token refusal margin by −7.10 versus −0.013 at the projector. Held-out-category and random-direction controls support the tested margin effect. | Rich's suggested audio refusal-direction starting experiment is already close to published work. The paper explicitly reports that changing this first-token margin does not reliably change full harmfulness behavior across models, and its interface null is limited to tested directions/subspaces. A refusal score or probe alone is therefore too weak an endpoint for our action question. |
| [Tracing Audio Grounding and Answer Selection in Audio LLMs](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04637) | On Qwen2-Audio and Qwen2.5-Omni multiple-choice tasks, silence/mismatched-audio controls test whether answers use acoustic evidence. Attention knockouts locate audio-to-choice influence in earlier/middle layers and choice-to-answer influence later; the principal layer endpoint is a probability over option letters. | This establishes a useful **grounding vs selection** split and a source-to-target attention intervention. It does not trace a free-form function script, a separately generated spoken reply, or tool authorization. Our assay should include silence and mismatched-natural-audio controls, but complete parsed action and heard answer must remain the primary outcomes. |
| [Heard but Not Heeded](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00727) and [Represented but Ignored](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19211) | Matched speech content with changed speaking style shows that paralinguistic information can be linearly decoded in the encoder while being underused in output. The latter paper adds targeted hidden-state interventions and distinguishes directional score movement from selective recovery of the correct class. | “The model contains a signal” and “the model uses it for this decision” are separate claims. Probe accuracy or an activation difference should be paired with a causal edit and full response. Existing studies cover prosody and class answers; our prospective target is an action script and speech channel from a voice assistant. |
| [Who Wins the Conflict?](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18924) | In multilingual audio/text conflicts, the authors use activation patching and causal circuit ablation to study text dominance, then back-patch audio activations to improve audio-supported closed-set answers. | Distinct audio/text pathways and multilingual conflict are prior art. VoiceChat's user input in our fixture is audio only; its text system prompt carries the tool schema. The separate question here is how one spoken authorization decision appears in its shared state, function head, text head and speech output. |
| [Heard Enough to Act](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34653) | A streaming spoken-agent study labels the earliest audio chunk at which a tool and its arguments are grounded, and trains a model to issue calls before turn end. Its endpoint is call timing and correctness. | Spoken tool selection and timing are prior art. Our fixed offline VoiceChat assay cannot make a claim about earliest actionable chunks; it can test whether a completed spoken request yields a correctly authorized function script and corresponding response. |

The [reviewed Wav2Vec2 saved-logit diagnostic](wav2vec2_margin_analysis_v1/INDEPENDENT_SAVED_LOGIT_REVIEW_GO.md) shows the same measurement trap in our own data: two underlying outside-word swaps moved donor-relative complete-reference CTC score past a fixed threshold, but no greedy transcript changed. This is synthetic ASR evidence only. The [VoiceChat source review](VOICECHAT_SOURCE_REVIEW.md) confirms the named model has distinct text and function heads reading a shared language state, while its stock offline helper makes two passes and injects a canned response. No live VoiceChat behavior has been observed in this project.

## Next falsifiable model-level hypothesis

**Hypothesis to test, not a finding.** With prompt, date, tool schema and decoding settings fixed, changing only the clear spoken authorization request from **ask before cancel** to **explicitly confirmed cancel** changes the complete raw function decision in an unmodified VoiceChat pass. The trace and controlled state swaps should identify whether that change is carried at the perception-to-language handoff, in the shared language state, or only in later function-token selection. The stronger security hypothesis is that some ask-before-cancel audio instead yields an unconfirmed cancel proposal. A text/function discrepancy is an observation to explain; it alone does not show acoustic dependence or different use of acoustic evidence by the heads.

1. Establish the frozen list-only, ask-before-cancel, confirmed-cancel and no-tool inputs in the [dual-head assay](VOICECHAT_DUAL_HEAD_ASSAY_PROTOCOL.md), with human listening, fixed date and seeded inert events. Predeclare the B-versus-C and list-only contrasts, pass-specific decision rubric, exact output and parser fields, and all-case denominator before inference. Record complete raw text/function streams and parser decisions separately for stock pass 1 and replay pass 2; only pass 2 has speech. Preserve unspliced audio and any optional/missing codec fields. Use silence and unrelated natural audio of matched duration as **grounding controls**, with their possible distribution shift reported, not as a substitute for clean intelligible inputs.
2. Verify that the audio contrast changes the measured perception output and shared state before inferring any downstream branch dependence. Locate the first action-discriminating function generation step from **matched prior token histories**. Test `asr_emb` authorization-phrase rows and the shared `last_hidden_state` before the two heads with self, full-donor, off-step/outside-region and norm-matched random controls. Report both immediate head margins and **complete parsed scripts and audible answers**. If histories differ before the selected step, report that divergence instead of forcing a fake alignment. Claim differential head use only if a controlled audio/state contrast produces a reproducible difference in the two heads' complete decisions, with normal on-hold speech excluded from false-promise scoring.
3. Evaluate separately: (a) a decodable signal, (b) a causal score effect, (c) a changed complete model decision, and (d) an unmodified input-reachable error from an ordinary intelligible waveform. The strong ask-before-cancel failure hypothesis is falsified on the tested cohort if no unmodified B input proposes unconfirmed cancellation; a score shift does not rescue it. For **this proposed tool-action scenario**, a concrete security finding needs an input-reachable unauthorized proposal and a separately demonstrated downstream consequence under an explicit harness. Other model security findings, such as confidentiality failures, need different evidence. Internal state edits establish mechanism, not an attacker capability.

This is a prospective synthesis. It does not predict that VoiceChat will fail, prove that text and function heads disagree, or turn a logged rejected cancellation into an executed tool action. The named model may speak an [on-hold message](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-NemotronLabs-VoiceChat-11B#model-overview) while a tool call is pending; that expected behavior is not a false promise to a user. The pinned English-only VoiceChat model also cannot establish multilingual transfer; that remains a separate cross-model test.
